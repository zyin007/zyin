Exp. 04In design

An RNA sensor that only fires inside a lung tumour

Team synthetic biology project (iGEM-style) · 2026–now

ProblemLung squamous cell carcinoma has few clean driver mutations, so targeted therapy works poorly and chemotherapy hits healthy cells too. Five-year survival sits around 18%.
DesignAn ADAR-based RNA sensor (RADAR): a synthetic mRNA carrying a guide sequence, a built-in UAG stop codon and a payload. The stop codon only gets edited out, and the payload only gets translated, when the target transcript is present in the cell.
My partChose DSG3 as the second biomarker for the AND gate and argued why the gate is needed: L1-FGGY cross-reacts with wild-type FGGY mRNA in normal lung, and DSG3 is absent there. Proposed split caspase-3 (p17/p12) as the effector, since neither half kills a cell alone. Worked out how to limit off-target ADAR editing with a shorter guide duplex and endogenous ADAR, validated by counting A-to-G changes in treated versus untreated sequencing.
Open questionsL1-FGGY expression hasn't been measured against the sensor's detection floor, and ADAR levels in these tumours are unknown.
